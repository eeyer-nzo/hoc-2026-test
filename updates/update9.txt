Title: The Prime Minister wants to raise taxes even more?
Date: 10 February 2026

<i> The Daily Telegraph </i>
In a move that has left backbenchers simmering and the nation on edge, the Prime Minister signaled this week that the "era of contribution" is far from over. Addressing a room of increasingly skeptical business leaders, he hinted that the "fiscal black hole" inherited at the start of the term has proved deeper than any 2024 forecast predicted. With the 2026 Spring Statement looming, the talk in Downing Street has shifted from "protecting working people" to a "National Resilience Levy", a move critics are already calling a blatant raid on middle-income earners. Despite the Chancellor's recent budget hiking taxes by £26bn, the PM insisted that further "difficult decisions" are the only way to insulate the NHS from future shocks, effectively rolling the pitch for a third consecutive year of record-breaking tax burdens. With no bills being passed since the recent parliament session started last month, the question remains: is the PM still suited to lead us through this nation's economic turmoil?
